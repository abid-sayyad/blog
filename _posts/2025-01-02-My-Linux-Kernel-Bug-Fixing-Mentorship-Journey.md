Hey there wanderer,

A couple of months ago I found out about the Linux kernel Bug Fixing Mentorship
Program. The program is aimed at helping you get into open source development
(contributing to Linux Kernel).

In my opinion, this is a very unique cohort, a very interesting experience, every
open source contributions enthusiast must try. It is unlike other cohorts or
programs, where you just fill a form, write emails and sometimes even pay a fee,
just to wait and see them being not very fun. Here your journey starts from the
very moment you decide to apply.

When I was applying for the program for the first time, earlier in 2024, for the
summer cohort of the mentorship, I was initially surprised by the number of
preliminary task required to be completed for a successful application. On a
careful read I could see what the task were trying to do. They were trying to
get me up to speed with the Linux Kernel devleopment process and also
understand the Linux kernel's fundamentals.

The tasks require you to submit an SOP, stating why you wish to join the program,
finish the LFD103 training course, and finish the coding challenges mentioned.
In the begining it might look a lot, but when you start progressing through the
LFD103 course, you see the coding challenges completing simulataneously.

The course is designed so beautifully that it helps you setup your dev environment,
learn the Linux Kernel contribution process, understand the working of the kernel
and explore beginner friendly subsytems.

The first time I applied I couldn't get in, due several issues at hand with my
laptop. Once I got them fixed up I reapplied for the Fall cohort and got in. The
beauty of this cohort also lies in the fact that it motivates you to contribute
on the go (i.e. while your just finishing the prelims tasks).

I started with trying to contributing to the documentation, by looking for spelling
fixes and 'html-makedocs' errors/ warnings tha occur while building the documentation.
I got to push some patches upstream during my task comletion time. Althoug none
of them were upstreamed, due to someone'd already existing patches, but I learnt
alot about how and where to start during that time.

Another fascinating part about this program is that it exposes you to the netwrork
of Linux kernel contributors out ther. There are people from all the levels
kernel newbies to seasoned maintainers. Each one of them looking forward to helping
you in starting up and keep going.

During my menteeship tenure, I experienced the warmth and welcoming nature of the
people who are contributing and developing the kernel. There were moments which
seemed unpleasant, but I had a huge hand, a keyboard and a screen in making it
that way. Regardless of that, I was guided and supported whenever I required.

A huge thanks to Shuah Khan, Javier Curassco Cruz, Anup Sharma, Nishant Menon,
Ayush Jain, Rob Herring, and everyone who were there to help and guide. These are
just a few of the names who you will always find, being out there to help you and
guide you whenever needed. They make you feel like your daily friend circle, but
with a lot of cool technoclogical contributions.

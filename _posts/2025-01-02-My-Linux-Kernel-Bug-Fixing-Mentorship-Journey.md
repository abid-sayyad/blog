# My Journey with the Linux Kernel Bug Fixing Mentorship Program  

## Hey there, wanderer,  

A couple of months ago, I discovered the **Linux Kernel Bug Fixing Mentorship Program**. This program is designed to help newcomers get into open-source development by contributing to the **Linux kernel**.  

In my opinion, this mentorship is a **unique and invaluable experience** that every open-source enthusiast should try. Unlike other programs where you just fill out a form, write emails, and sometimes even pay a fee—only to find them uninspiring—this one engages you from the very moment you decide to apply.  

When I first applied for the program in early **2024** for the **summer cohort**, I was surprised by the number of preliminary tasks required for a successful application. However, upon careful reading, I realized their purpose—they were meant to **familiarize me with the Linux kernel development process** and build a strong foundation in **kernel fundamentals**.  

## The Application Process  

The application process involves:  
- **Submitting a Statement of Purpose (SOP)** explaining why you wish to join.  
- **Completing the LFD103 training course.**  
- **Solving the required coding challenges.**  

At first, it might seem overwhelming, but as you progress through the **LFD103 course**, you’ll notice the coding challenges getting completed simultaneously.  

The course is **brilliantly structured**—helping you set up your **development environment**, learn the **Linux kernel contribution process**, understand **how the kernel works**, and explore **beginner-friendly subsystems**.  

## Reapplying & Getting Accepted  

The first time I applied, I **didn’t get in** due to some issues with my laptop. But once I fixed them, I **reapplied for the Fall cohort**—and this time, I got in!  

One of the **best aspects of this mentorship** is that it **motivates you to contribute while you’re still completing the preliminary tasks**.  

## My First Contributions  

I started contributing to the **documentation** by looking for spelling mistakes and fixing `html-makedocs` errors/warnings that occurred while building the docs. I also pushed some patches **upstream** during the application period.  

Although none of my patches were merged (since someone else had already submitted similar fixes), I **learned a lot** about how and where to start.  

## The Power of Community  

Another fascinating part of this program is the **network of Linux kernel contributors** you get exposed to. From complete **newbies** to **seasoned maintainers**, everyone is willing to help you get started and keep going.  

During my mentorship tenure, I **experienced firsthand the warmth and welcoming nature** of the kernel community. Sure, there were moments of frustration, but in the end, I had my **hands on the keyboard and a screen in front of me**—and I made it through. Whenever I needed guidance, there was always someone ready to help.  

## A Huge Thank You  

I want to express my gratitude to **Shuah Khan, Javier Carrasco Cruz, Anup Sharma, Nishant Menon, Ayush Jain, Rob Herring,** and **everyone else** who was there to support and guide me.  

These individuals aren’t just mentors—they make you feel like they’re part of your **everyday friend circle**, but with **insanely cool technological contributions**.
